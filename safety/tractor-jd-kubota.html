John Deere / Kubota Tractor

SWP Issue: Nov 2026 Review: Nov 2027
Description: Operating a Tractor
Make / Model: John Deere 3520 / Kubota L5740
⚠ DO NOT operate this equipment unless you have been fully trained in its safe use and operation, have read the user manual, and have conducted a risk assessment using the Task Tracker QR system.

1. Hardware and Guards:

  • Inspect all hardware (bolts, nuts, and fasteners) for tightness. Replace worn, damaged, or missing components before operation.
  • Ensure all guards are correctly fitted and functional. This includes the tractor master shield, driveline shields, and PTO guards. Never operate the machine without guards in place.

2. Safety Features:

  • Check the seatbelt for wear or fraying. Ensure the Roll Over Protective Structure (ROPS) and Falling Object Protective Structure (FOPS) (if fitted) are intact and locked into position.
  • Ensure the PTO guard rotates freely and is undamaged.

3. Tyres and Wheels:

  • Verify tyre pressure matches the manufacturer’s recommended levels. Incorrect inflation can lead to blowouts or instability.
  • Inspect tyres for cuts, cracks, bubbles, or wear. Ensure rims are free from damage and lug nuts are secure.
  • Use a clip-on chuck with an extension hose for tyre inflation to avoid standing directly in front of the tyre. If a safety cage is available, it must be used.

4. Hydraulic and Pneumatic Systems:

  • Check all hydraulic hoses, connectors, and cylinders for leaks or wear. Leaking fluid can cause environmental contamination and reduce system efficiency.
  • Ensure linkage arms and stabilisers are free-moving and undamaged.

5. Safety Signs and Decals:

  • Ensure all safety decals are visible, legible, and located near potential danger points. Replace missing or damaged decals.

6. Fuel Safety:

  • Refuel only when the engine is off and has cooled. Ensure the area is free from ignition sources such as sparks, cigarettes, or open flames.
  • Refuel outdoors or in a well-ventilated area. Clean up spilled fuel immediately and store fuel safely away from the worksite.

7. Work Area Assessment:

  • Walk through the worksite to identify hazards such as uneven surfaces, holes, embankments, or unstable ground.
  • Communicate the work plan with team members to prevent overlapping tasks or accidents.

8. PPE (Personal Protective Equipment):

  • Wear safety goggles or glasses with side shields, a hard hat, long trousers, and enclosed footwear.
  • Use hearing protection, such as earplugs, to prevent long-term hearing damage.

9. Preliminary Safety Checks:

  • Test operator presence controls, safety interlocks, parking brakes, and other critical systems.
  • Ensure all lights, reflectors, and warning devices are operational, especially if operating near roads.

10. Road Use Preparation:

  • Lock brake pedals together before driving on public roads.
  • Use hazard lights and ensure the vehicle is registered and roadworthy.

2. Hitching Implements

Hitching implements safely is critical for preventing accidents or damage during operation. Follow these steps in maximum detail

1. Preparation:

  • Park the tractor on level, stable ground. Disengage the PTO, lower any existing implements, and turn off the engine.
  • Apply the parking brake and remove the ignition key before starting the hitching process.

2. Alignment:

  • Reverse the tractor slowly, using a spotter if visibility is restricted, to align the lower hitch arms with the implement's hitch pins.
  • Ensure the hitch points are free of debris, rust, or obstructions.

3. Securing the Connection:

  • Attach the lower lift arms to the implement’s hitch pins. Insert lynch pins or safety clips to lock the arms securely.
  • Adjust the top link and secure it to the implement’s top hitch point. Tighten to prevent unwanted movement during operation.
  • Use stabiliser bars or chains to limit lateral movement. Check that stabilisers are securely tightened.

4. PTO Shaft Connection (if applicable):

  • Inspect the PTO shaft for wear, debris, or grease build-up. Clean and grease it as necessary.
  • Align the shaft with the tractor's PTO stub and engage the locking collar or pin. Confirm the connection is secure.
  • Ensure the PTO guard rotates freely and covers the entire driveline.

5. Hydraulic Connections (if applicable):

  • Clean hydraulic couplings to prevent contamination.
  • Attach hydraulic hoses to the appropriate tractor connections and test for leaks.

6. Final Checks:

  • Slowly raise and lower the implement using the hydraulics to ensure proper operation.
  • Test for stability and secure connections before starting work.

3. Operation

General Operating Safety

  • Use three points of contact when mounting or dismounting the tractor. Never jump on or off.
  • Always start the tractor from the operator’s seat, with the transmission in neutral or park. Never bypass starter circuitry.
  • Ensure no one is standing near the tractor or implement before starting the engine.

Load Management

  • Distribute loads evenly in buckets or attachments to avoid instability.
  • Keep loads low and close to the tractor while travelling to maintain balance.

Terrain and Slope Safety

  • Drive straight up or down slopes; avoid turning across them.
  • Use low gear and engine braking when descending steep grades.
  • Avoid operating near ditches, holes, or embankments that could collapse.

4. Towing Loads Safely

Proper towing procedures reduce risks of loss of control or tipping.

1. Approved Hitches Only:

  • Use only manufacturer-approved hitches. Never attach loads to parts of the tractor not designed for towing.

2. Weight Limits:

  • Do not exceed the total towed weight specified in the tractor’s manual. This includes the weight of the pulling machine, ballast, and operator.
  • Add counterweights or wheel weights to the tractor if required to maintain stability.

3. Speed and Stopping:

  • Travel slowly, especially when towing heavy loads.
  • Allow extra time and distance for stopping. Avoid sudden stops or changes in direction.

4. Slopes:

  • Reduce load weight when towing on slopes.
  • Do not attempt to back up a slope if the towed load is too heavy. Instead, reduce the load or avoid the slope entirely.

5. Bystander Safety:

  • Never allow anyone, especially children, to ride on towed equipment.

5. Post-Operation

1. Shutting Down:

  • Park the tractor on level ground, disengage the PTO, and lower all implements.
  • Turn off the engine, apply the parking brake, and remove the ignition key.

2. Inspection and Cleaning:

  • Remove any debris from the tractor, attachments, and implements.
  • Inspect for wear, leaks, or damage and report issues to a supervisor.
  • Wash the exterior and cab, and dispose of waste in compliance with environmental regulations.
