JD 2550 Greens Mower

SWP Issue: Nov 2026 Review: Nov 2027
Description: Walk-Behind Precision Greens Mowing
Make / Model: John Deere 2550 PrecisionCut
⚠ DO NOT operate this equipment unless you have been fully trained in its safe use and operation, have read the user manual, and have conducted a risk assessment using the Task Tracker QR system.

1. Potential Hazards

  • Rotating cutting reel: The reel spins at high speed and can sever fingers or toes on contact. Never reach under the cutting unit while the engine is running.
  • Bedknife laceration: The stationary bedknife is razor-sharp and can cause deep cuts during height-of-cut adjustment or reel backlapping.
  • Noise exposure: Engine noise exceeds 85 dB(A), which causes hearing damage with prolonged unprotected exposure.
  • Vibration injury: Extended operation transmits hand-arm vibration through the handlebars, risking Hand-Arm Vibration Syndrome (HAVS).
  • Fuel and oil spills: Petrol is flammable and toxic; oil on greens surfaces causes turf damage and environmental contamination.
  • Slope instability: Greens surrounds and approaches may have slopes where the mower can slide sideways, especially on dewy or wet turf.
  • Struck-by risk from golfers: Greens are active play areas; errant golf balls travel at speed and can strike operators.
  • Manual handling strain: Lifting the mower on/off trailers or over kerbs risks back and shoulder injury.
  • Hot engine surfaces: Exhaust and engine block reach temperatures that cause burns on contact.
  • Ejected debris: Small stones, irrigation head covers, or ball markers can be thrown by the reel.

2. Required Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

  • Steel-capped or composite-toe boots with non-slip soles (mandatory).
  • Hearing protection: earmuffs or moulded ear plugs rated to Class 5 or above (mandatory).
  • Safety glasses or wrap-around eye protection (mandatory).
  • Sun hat and SPF 50+ sunscreen applied before starting work.
  • Nitrile gloves when handling fuel, oil, or performing backlapping.
  • High-visibility vest when mowing near roads, car parks, or during early morning starts.

3. Pre-Operational Checks

a. Machine Inspection

  • Complete pre-start checklist via the Task Tracker QR system before each use.
  • Check engine oil level on the dipstick; top up with the specified grade if below the minimum mark.
  • Inspect the cutting reel for nicks, chips, or bent blades. A damaged reel tears turf instead of cutting it.
  • Verify bedknife-to-reel contact by sliding a strip of paper through the gap; it should cut cleanly across the full width.
  • Check height-of-cut (HOC) bench setting matches the superintendent's daily instruction (typically 2.5-3.5 mm on greens).
  • Inspect roller condition for flat spots or buildup; clean rollers affect cut quality and prevent tracking marks.
  • Check fuel level and fill from approved fuel container on a hard surface, never on the green.
  • Verify grass catcher is fitted securely with no tears or holes that would drop clippings on the green.

b. Safety Systems

  • Test the operator-presence control (OPC): release the bail lever and confirm the reel stops within 3 seconds.
  • Check throttle return: release the throttle and confirm engine returns to idle.
  • Inspect drive belt condition for cracks or fraying.

c. Work Area

  • Walk the green before mowing to remove debris, ball markers, broken tees, divot tools, and irrigation head covers.
  • Check for irrigation heads sitting proud of the surface that could contact the bedknife.
  • Confirm golfers are aware of maintenance activity; use two-way radio to coordinate with the pro shop if the course is open.
  • Check dew or frost conditions; mowing on frosted turf causes cell damage and discolouration.

4. Operation

a. Start-Up

  • Position the mower on the green collar or apron, not on the putting surface, before starting the engine.
  • Set the throttle to half, choke on (if cold), pull the starter cord smoothly.
  • Allow the engine to warm for 30 seconds before engaging the reel.
  • Engage the reel drive on the collar and walk onto the green at mowing speed; never start the reel while stationary on the green.

b. During Operation

  • Mow at a consistent walking pace. Speeding up or slowing down causes uneven cut height.
  • Follow the daily mowing direction set by the superintendent to manage grain and promote upright growth.
  • Overlap each pass by approximately 25 mm to prevent missed strips.
  • Make clean-up passes around the perimeter; lift the reel before turning to avoid scuffing.
  • Empty the grass catcher before it is more than three-quarters full; an overfull catcher drops clippings on the green.
  • Dispose of clippings in the designated area, not on bunker surrounds or native areas.

c. Golf Course Specific Rules

  • Yield to golfers: stop mowing and move off the green when players are preparing to putt or approaching the green.
  • Do not mow across sprinkler heads, valve boxes, or pin placements.
  • Avoid mowing within 300 mm of hole edges to prevent collar damage.
  • Report any scalping, hydraulic oil leaks on the green, or unusual reel noise immediately to the superintendent.

d. General Safety

  • Never leave the engine running unattended.
  • Never attempt to clear a blockage or adjust HOC while the engine is running.
  • Maintain three-point contact when lifting mowers on/off trailers.
  • On slopes, mow across the slope rather than up and down where practical.

5. Emergency Procedures

a. Fire

  • Shut off the engine immediately.
  • Move the mower away from the green surface if safe to do so.
  • Use the nearest fire extinguisher (dry chemical) on the utility vehicle.
  • Call 000 if the fire cannot be controlled. Notify the superintendent via two-way radio.

b. Injury

  • Stop the machine, disengage the reel, and turn off the engine.
  • Administer first aid. The nearest first aid kit is on each utility vehicle and in the maintenance facility.
  • For cuts from the reel or bedknife, apply pressure and elevate the wound. These cuts can be deep and require stitches.
  • Call 000 for serious injuries. The site address is 825 Cranbourne-Frankston Rd, Cranbourne VIC 3977.
  • Report all incidents to the superintendent immediately and complete an incident report in Task Tracker.

6. Post-Operational Procedures

  • Disengage the reel and allow it to stop completely before leaving the green.
  • Transport the mower back to the maintenance facility on the utility vehicle; do not drive the mower on paths or roads.
  • Wash down the mower thoroughly at the wash-down bay, paying attention to the reel, bedknife, and rollers.
  • Empty and clean the grass catcher.
  • Check the reel and bedknife for damage after each use.
  • Top up fuel for the next operator if the tank is below half.
  • Report any faults, unusual noises, or performance issues in Task Tracker.
  • Park the mower in its designated bay in the machinery shed.

7. Maintenance and Servicing

  • Daily: clean machine, check oil, inspect reel and bedknife contact.
  • Weekly: grease zerks per the lubrication chart, check belt tension, inspect air filter.
  • Backlapping: performed as directed by the superintendent (typically weekly or fortnightly) using approved compound.
  • Reel grinding and bedknife replacement: performed by qualified technician or in-house mechanic per the manufacturer schedule.
  • All servicing to be recorded in Task Tracker with hours, parts used, and technician name.
  • Only genuine or approved aftermarket parts to be used. Consult the superintendent before substituting parts.

8. Relevant Legislation

  • Occupational Health and Safety Act 2004 (Vic) - employer and employee duties.
  • Occupational Health and Safety Regulations 2017 (Vic) - plant and equipment, noise, manual handling.
  • AS/NZS 4024.1 Safety of Machinery series.
  • EPA Victoria noise regulations - Environment Protection Act 2017.
  • Dangerous Goods (Storage and Handling) Regulations 2012 (Vic) for fuel storage.

9. Record Keeping

  • Pre-start checklists completed daily via Task Tracker QR system.
  • All faults, repairs, and servicing recorded in Task Tracker.
  • Incident and near-miss reports submitted through Task Tracker.
  • Training records maintained for all operators, including competency sign-off by the superintendent.
  • Height-of-cut records maintained in the daily greens management log.
