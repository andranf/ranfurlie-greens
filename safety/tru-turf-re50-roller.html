Tru Turf RE50 Electric Golf Roller

SWP Issue: Nov 2026 Review: Nov 2027
Description: Operating a Greens Roller
Make / Model: Tru Turf RE50 Electric Golf Roller
⚠ DO NOT operate this equipment unless you have been fully trained in its safe use and operation, have read the user manual, and have conducted a risk assessment using the Task Tracker QR system.

1. Familiarise yourself with the operator’s manual, machine controls, and all safety guidelines.

2. Complete a pre-use risk assessment via the task tracker QR code system to confirm competency.

Inspection of Worksite

1. Examine the area for potential hazards such as rocks, debris, sprinkler heads, or wet patches.

2. Assess the terrain for uneven surfaces, steep slopes, or soft ground that could cause instability.

Equipment Checks

1. Tighten all nuts, bolts, and grub screws. Replace or reapply Loctite sealants where seals are broken.

2. Verify the batteries are fully charged using the charger meter for accurate status. Ensure no overheating or wear on charging leads.

3. Inspect the steering joystick for looseness and test the foot pedal’s return to the neutral position.

4. Confirm the draw bar locking mechanism is secure, with the safety pin properly in place.

5. Inspect all safety decals and operational labels, ensuring they are intact and legible.

6. Check tyre pressure (15 psi) and avoid over-inflation to maintain stability.

7. Lubricate all required components, including:

  • Bearings (apply lightly).
  • Drive chain (use appropriate chain lubricant).

8. Verify the emergency stop system is functioning correctly.

Communication and Coordination

1. Notify other staff of planned work locations to ensure operational areas are clear.

2. Plan efficient movement between greens to reduce unnecessary machine wear.

3. Document any concerns or irregularities noted during the pre-operation inspection.

Operation Procedures

Towing and Movement

1. Use a suitable towing vehicle, ensuring the roller is properly attached and locked.

2. Travel at a safe towing speed (not exceeding 7 km/h or golf buggy speed).

3. Avoid towing the roller across rough or uneven terrain at high speeds.

Starting the Roller

1. Position the roller on the fringe of the green before setup; never tow directly onto the green.

2. Start the roller on a stable, level surface and drive gently onto the green.

Rolling Greens

1. Roll greens in any direction while ensuring no crease lines are left on the surface.

2. Utilise regenerative braking for smooth stopping, allowing adequate distance to stop safely.

3. Monitor surroundings continuously to avoid collisions with golfers, maintenance staff, or obstacles.

Operational Safety Practices

1. Operate cautiously on slopes, always approaching perpendicularly to minimise the risk of rollover.

2. Avoid use during heavy rain, fog, or other low-visibility conditions.

3. Never leave the roller unattended while in operation.

4. Passengers are not permitted on the machine at any time.

Post-Operation Checklist

1. Clean grass and debris from rollers, checking all components for damage during the cleaning process.

2. Inspect for wear, such as loosened bolts or damaged decals, and address issues promptly.

3. Park the roller in its designated storage area and connect it to the charging device.

4. Log all maintenance needs or unusual performance observations in the equipment logbook.

Legislative Compliance

1. Occupational Health and Safety Act 2004 (Vic): Adhere to legal requirements for safe plant operation and workplace practices.

2. Occupational Health and Safety Regulations 2017 (Vic): Follow safety standards for plant, machinery, and manual handling.

3. Electricity Safety Act 1998 (Vic): Ensure compliance with guidelines for battery-operated equipment.

Training and Competency

1. Operators must complete initial training covering machine operation, hazard identification, and legislative requirements.

2. Supervisors must assess and verify operator competency through supervised tests.

3. Regular refresher training is mandatory to address equipment updates, procedural changes, or identified safety concerns.

Risk Management

1. Conduct periodic reviews of the Safe Work Procedure to incorporate updates in equipment, site conditions, or legislation.

2. Perform a Job Safety Analysis (JSA) for tasks involving higher risks or unique environmental conditions.

3. Actively encourage reporting of near-misses, hazards, or risks to foster a culture of continuous safety improvement.
