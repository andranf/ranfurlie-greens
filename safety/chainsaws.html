Chainsaws

SWP Issue: Nov 2026 Review: Nov 2027
Description: Chainsaw Operation
Make / Model: Stihl Petrol and Kress Battery Chainsaws
⚠ DO NOT operate this equipment unless you have been fully trained in its safe use and operation, have read the user manual, and have conducted a risk assessment using the Task Tracker QR system.

1. Potential Hazards

  • Kickback: The most dangerous chainsaw hazard. When the tip of the guide bar contacts an object, the saw can kick back toward the operator with extreme force, causing fatal head, neck, and body lacerations.
  • Laceration: The chain moves at high speed and can cause deep, life-threatening cuts to any part of the body.
  • Falling timber: Cut branches and trunks can fall unpredictably, striking the operator or bystanders.
  • Noise exposure: Petrol chainsaws typically exceed 100 dB(A). Immediate hearing damage is possible without protection.
  • Vibration: Significant hand-arm vibration leading to HAVS with regular use.
  • Exhaust fumes: CO poisoning risk with petrol models in enclosed areas.
  • Fuel fire: Petrol refuelling near hot exhaust.
  • Eye injury: Wood chips, sawdust, and bark fly during cutting.

2. Required Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

  • Chainsaw chaps or trousers with cut-protection (mandatory - AS/NZS 4453.3).
  • Steel-capped boots with chainsaw protection or chainsaw boots (mandatory).
  • Hearing protection rated Class 5 (mandatory).
  • Safety glasses or mesh face shield (mandatory).
  • Hard hat (mandatory when risk of falling branches).
  • Chainsaw gloves with cut protection (mandatory).
  • High-visibility vest.
  • First aid kit within reach at all times.

3. Pre-Operational Checks

a. Machine Inspection

  • Complete pre-start via Task Tracker QR.
  • Inspect chain tension: should snap back when pulled. Not too loose (derailment risk) or too tight (overheating).
  • Check chain sharpness; a dull chain requires excessive force and increases kickback risk.
  • Inspect guide bar for burrs or damage.
  • Check chain brake function: must engage with wrist pressure on the front guard.
  • Check throttle lock and trigger interlock.
  • For petrol: check fuel and bar oil levels.
  • For battery: check battery condition.

b. Work Area

  • Assess the tree/branch: lean direction, dead wood, hung-up branches, power lines within two tree lengths.
  • Establish a drop zone and escape route (45 degrees behind the fall direction).
  • No personnel within two tree lengths of the felling zone.
  • If the tree requires climbing, a professional arborist must be engaged (not maintenance staff).

4. Operation

a. Start-Up

  • Start the chainsaw on the ground, not in the air.
  • Engage chain brake. Use the correct starting position (ground start or drop start).
  • Allow engine to warm. Disengage chain brake only when ready to cut.

b. During Operation

  • Always grip the saw firmly with both hands. Left thumb must wrap around the front handle.
  • Stand to the side of the cutting line, never directly behind the bar.
  • Do not cut above shoulder height.
  • Watch the chain tip at all times to prevent kickback.
  • Use bore cuts and retreat cuts for large timber; never force the bar.
  • If the chain binds, stop the engine, do not yank the saw free.
  • Never use a chainsaw alone. A second person must be present and within communication range.

c. Golf Course Specific Rules

  • Only trained and competent operators may use chainsaws. Minimum qualification: attend a chainsaw safety course.
  • Do not fell trees without superintendent authorisation and a specific risk assessment.
  • Pruning is the typical chainsaw task on the course; felling requires arborist assessment.
  • Keep golfers and public well clear; close the area if needed.

5. Emergency Procedures

a. Fire

  • Engage chain brake. Set saw on ground. Extinguisher. Call 000.

b. Injury

  • Engage chain brake. Put saw down. Apply direct pressure to any laceration. Call 000 immediately.
  • Chainsaw lacerations are deep and can be life-threatening. Do not delay calling 000.
  • Site: 825 Cranbourne-Frankston Rd, Cranbourne VIC 3977.
  • Report in Task Tracker.

c. Equipment-Specific Emergency

  • If a tree hangs up during felling, do not attempt to free it by cutting. Secure the area and use machinery to pull it clear.

6. Post-Operational Procedures

  • Engage chain brake. Allow to cool.
  • Clean the saw, clear sawdust from the air intake.
  • Check chain tension after the saw cools.
  • Replenish bar oil and fuel.
  • Store in a ventilated area. Report faults in Task Tracker.

7. Maintenance and Servicing

  • After each use: clean, check chain, check bar oil.
  • Regularly: sharpen chain (or use the Tecomec sharpener), inspect guide bar.
  • Service per manufacturer schedule. Record in Task Tracker.

8. Relevant Legislation

  • Occupational Health and Safety Act 2004 (Vic).
  • Occupational Health and Safety Regulations 2017 (Vic).
  • AS/NZS 4024.1 Safety of Machinery series.
  • EPA Victoria noise regulations - Environment Protection Act 2017.
  • Dangerous Goods (Storage and Handling) Regulations 2012 (Vic) where applicable.

9. Record Keeping

  • Pre-start checklists completed daily via Task Tracker QR system.
  • All faults, repairs, and servicing recorded in Task Tracker.
  • Incident and near-miss reports submitted through Task Tracker.
  • Training records maintained for all operators with superintendent sign-off.
