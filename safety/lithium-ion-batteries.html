Lithium-Ion Batteries & Equipment

SWP Issue: Nov 2026 Review: Nov 2027
Description: Lithium Ion Batteries and Equipment
Make / Model: Use of Lithium Ion Equipment
⚠ DO NOT operate this equipment unless you have been fully trained in its safe use and operation, have read the user manual, and have conducted a risk assessment using the Task Tracker QR system.

1. Potential Hazards

  • Fire Risk: Lithium batteries can overheat, ignite, or explode if damaged, overcharged, or short-circuited.
  • Chemical Exposure: Batteries may leak harmful chemicals that can cause skin irritation, respiratory issues, or burns.
  • Electrical Shock: Improper handling of batteries or chargers can result in electric shock.
  • Crush Injuries: Batteries or equipment may cause injuries if dropped or mishandled.
  • Thermal Burns: Direct contact with overheated batteries or equipment can result in burns.
  • Fume Inhalation: Overheating or damaged batteries may release toxic fumes.
  • Explosion Risk: Faulty or damaged batteries can release gases that ignite, causing explosions.
  • Environmental Impact: Incorrect disposal of lithium batteries can harm the environment.

2. Required Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

  • Mandatory:
  • Safety glasses with side shields.
  • Chemical-resistant gloves when handling damaged or leaking batteries.
  • Protective clothing (long sleeves and pants).
  • Fire-resistant gloves when handling overheated batteries.
  • Recommended:
  • Dust mask or respirator (when dealing with fumes).
  • Steel-capped boots for handling large equipment.

3. Pre-Operational Checks

1. Equipment Inspection:

  • Check the battery and equipment for physical damage such as cracks, swelling, or leaks.
  • Ensure all connections are secure and free from corrosion.
  • Verify that the charger is compatible with the battery and complies with manufacturer specifications.

2. Area Preparation:

  • Designate a well-ventilated area for charging and operating lithium-powered equipment.
  • Keep the area free of flammable materials and liquids.
  • Ensure fire extinguishers rated for lithium battery fires (Class D) are easily accessible.

3. Charging Setup:

  • Inspect the charger and power cord for damage.
  • Ensure the charging station is dry, cool (15–25°C), and away from direct sunlight.
  • Use only the manufacturer-approved charger for the specific battery.

4. Documentation and Competency:

  • Verify that the operator has completed training on the safe use and handling of lithium-powered equipment.
  • Record pre-use inspections in the logbook.

4. Operation

1. Starting the Equipment:

  • Confirm the battery is fully seated and properly secured in the equipment.
  • Avoid operating equipment near heat sources or open flames.
  • Check all controls for smooth operation before commencing work.

2. During Use:

  • Regularly monitor the battery’s temperature. Stop operation immediately if the battery becomes excessively hot.
  • Do not use equipment that emits unusual smells, smoke, or sparks.
  • Maintain a safe distance from water or excessive moisture to prevent short circuits.
  • Keep bystanders at least 5 metres away during operation.

3. Emergency Shutdown:

  • If the equipment malfunctions, immediately turn it off and disconnect the battery.
  • Move the equipment to a safe location away from flammable materials.

5. Post-Operational Procedures

1. Shutdown:

  • Turn off the equipment and remove the battery after use.
  • Place the battery on a non-flammable, heat-resistant surface for cooling.

2. Storage:

  • Store batteries in a cool, dry, and well-ventilated area.
  • Ensure batteries are kept away from flammable materials and direct sunlight.
  • Avoid storing batteries in fully discharged or fully charged states; maintain a charge level between 40–80%.

3. Cleaning:

  • Clean the equipment and battery terminals with a dry, lint-free cloth.
  • Do not use water or solvents.

6. Maintenance and Disposal

1. Maintenance:

  • Regularly inspect batteries for signs of wear or damage.
  • Replace batteries that show swelling, leaking, or unusual behaviour.
  • Keep chargers clean and free of debris.

2. Disposal:

  • Dispose of damaged or end-of-life batteries at an authorised recycling facility.
  • Never dispose of lithium batteries in general waste, as they pose a fire risk in landfills.

7. Emergency Procedures

1. Fire Response:

  • Evacuate the area and call emergency services.
  • Use a Class D fire extinguisher or sand to extinguish small fires.
  • Do not use water, as it can exacerbate lithium fires.

2. Chemical Exposure:

  • If skin contact occurs, rinse immediately with water for at least 15 minutes.
  • For inhalation of fumes, move to fresh air and seek medical attention.
  • Wear PPE to handle leaking batteries.

3. Overheating or Swelling Battery:

  • Turn off the equipment and move the battery to a safe, non-flammable area.
  • Do not attempt to puncture or open the battery.
  • Allow the battery to cool naturally in a well-ventilated space.

4. Explosion Risk:

  • In the event of an explosion, evacuate the area and contact emergency services.
  • Do not attempt to re-enter the area until cleared by professionals.

8. Relevant Legislation and Standards

  • Occupational Health and Safety Act 2004 (Vic): Employer and operator obligations for a safe workplace.
  • Dangerous Goods (Storage and Handling) Regulations 2012 (Vic): Guidelines for storing and handling lithium batteries.
  • AS/NZS IEC 60950.1: Safety standards for battery-powered equipment.
  • EPA Victoria Guidelines: Proper disposal methods for hazardous materials, including lithium batteries.
