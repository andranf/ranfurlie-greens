Track Loader

SWP Issue: Nov 2026 Review: Nov 2027
Description: Safe Operation of Track Loader
Make / Model: New Holland C227 Track Loader
⚠ DO NOT operate this equipment unless you have been fully trained in its safe use and operation, have read the user manual, and have conducted a risk assessment using the Task Tracker QR system.

1. Equipment Inspection

  • Follow the user manual for pre-start checks:
  • Check fuel, hydraulic fluid, and engine oil levels.
  • Inspect tracks for wear or damage, especially if transitioning between paved and turf areas.
  • Ensure safety devices (e.g., seatbelts, alarms, and control levers) are functional.
  • Verify the Roll-Overprotective Structure (ROPS) is intact.

2. Course Area Inspection

  • Walk the work area to identify potential hazards:
  • Mark sprinkler heads, irrigation systems, and drainage points to prevent damage.
  • Assess ground stability, especially near bunkers, greens, and slopes.
  • Clear the area of golf balls, debris, and equipment that could be struck.
  • Be aware of proximity to members and guests; communicate closures of work zones if necessary.

3. Operator Readiness

  • Confirm that operators:
  • Are fully trained and aware of golf course-specific protocols.
  • Are fit for duty and free from impairing substances.

4. Documentation

  • Record pre-start checks in Task Tracker or equivalent.
  • Review and acknowledge the Safe Work Method Statement (SWMS) or this SWP.

Operational Guidelines

1. Starting the Loader

  • Park the loader on a stable, level area to avoid damage to turf.
  • Inspect the work area for golfers or other personnel.
  • Fasten seatbelt and adjust the seat for safe operation.
  • Allow the engine to warm up before starting work.

2. Safe Practices on the Golf Course

  • Operate at low speeds to minimize turf damage and ensure safety.
  • Avoid tight turns on turf to prevent rutting or tearing.
  • Stay on designated paths or areas approved for machinery; avoid greens and tee boxes unless necessary.
  • Use plywood or mats when operating in sensitive areas to distribute weight.
  • Communicate with nearby players or staff before entering work zones.

3. Handling Attachments

  • Secure attachments (e.g., buckets, forks) before operation.
  • Use tools appropriate for the task to avoid unnecessary damage to turf or sand traps.

4. Hydraulic Safety

  • Inspect all hydraulic hoses and fittings before operation.
  • Avoid placing hands or body near pressurized lines.

5. Environmental Considerations

  • Protect water features and native vegetation by maintaining buffer zones.
  • Use spill kits immediately for any leaks, especially near waterways or wetlands.

Post-Operational Procedures

1. Shutdown

  • Park the loader on a level area, preferably a paved or designated maintenance zone.
  • Lower attachments to the ground to stabilize the machine.
  • Turn off the engine and remove the key.

2. Cleaning and Inspection

  • Clean tracks and attachments to remove sand, grass, or debris.
  • Inspect the loader for new damage or leaks and report any findings.
  • Record post-operational checks in Task Tracker or equivalent.

3. Storage

  • Store the loader in a secure location within the maintenance area.
  • Lock all hatches and compartments to prevent unauthorized use.

Maintenance and Servicing

1. Scheduled Maintenance

  • Conduct maintenance per the manufacturer’s recommendations.
  • Record servicing in a designated log.

2. Pre-Service Preparations

  • Clean the loader thoroughly to ensure ease of maintenance.
  • Report any performance issues before scheduled servicing.

3. Post-Service Records

  • Verify and document all completed servicing.
  • Retain service records for compliance and audits.

Emergency Procedures

1. Machine Rollover

  • Remain inside the cab with the seatbelt fastened until the loader stops.
  • Shut off the engine and call for assistance.

2. Interaction with Utilities

  • Immediately stop operations if an irrigation line or utility is struck.
  • Notify the golf course maintenance team and utility service provider.

3. Injury Response

  • Provide first aid and call emergency services for severe injuries.
  • Document the incident and notify the course superintendent.
