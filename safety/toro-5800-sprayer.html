Toro 5800 Boom Sprayer

SWP Issue: Nov 2026 Review: Nov 2027
Description: Applying Pesticides - Boom Spraying
Make / Model: Toro 5800
⚠ DO NOT operate this equipment unless you have been fully trained in its safe use and operation, have read the user manual, and have conducted a risk assessment using the Task Tracker QR system.

1. Potential Hazards

  • Chemical Exposure: Risk of skin contact, inhalation, or ingestion of herbicides causing irritation, poisoning, or long-term health issues.
  • Incorrect PPE Usage: Failure to follow label-specific PPE requirements may result in severe health risks.
  • Environmental Contamination: Herbicides may contaminate watercourses, soil, or non-target vegetation if misapplied or spilled.
  • Drift and Runoff: Unintended spray drift or chemical runoff can harm neighbouring ecosystems.
  • Equipment Failure: Faulty or poorly maintained sprayers can lead to leaks, uneven application, or operator injury.
  • Slip, Trip, and Fall: Wet or uneven terrain during spraying increases the risk of accidents.
  • Reputation and Legal Risks: Non-compliance with herbicide laws and poor record-keeping can lead to fines, community complaints, or reputational damage.

2. Required Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

PPE requirements must always be determined by consulting the herbicide label and Safety Data Sheet (SDS) prior to mixing or application. Typical PPE includes:

  • Mandatory:
  • Chemical-resistant gloves.
  • Long-sleeve shirts and long pants.
  • Safety goggles or a face shield.
  • Respirator or dust mask (if indicated on the label).
  • Steel-capped boots with slip-resistant soles.
  • Additional PPE as Per Label Instructions:
  • Aprons or coveralls for certain concentrated herbicides.
  • Arm shields or specific footwear if required by the label.

3. Pre-Operational Checks

1. Read and Understand the Label:

  • Always read the entire label before opening the herbicide container.
  • Note specific PPE requirements, mixing ratios, application rates, and restrictions (e.g., wind speed, temperature).
  • Follow all label instructions to ensure compliance and effectiveness.

2. Inspection of the Toro 5800 Sprayer:

  • Inspect the boom structure for damage, ensuring it is secure and stable.
  • Check hoses, nozzles, and tanks for leaks, cracks, or blockages.
  • Verify the GPS-based control system is functioning, as this ensures precision in spray application and minimises drift.
  • Ensure pressure gauges, valves, and nozzles are operational.
  • Confirm that previous herbicides have been fully cleaned from the tank to avoid cross-contamination.

3. Calibration:

  • Use Spray Diary software to calibrate the sprayer based on label specifications for boom height, nozzle flow rate, pressure, and ground speed.
  • Perform a test spray using the Toro 5800’s adjustable boom settings to verify consistent application across all nozzles.

4. Weather Conditions:

  • Do not spray in winds above 15 km/h to prevent drift.
  • Avoid spraying in temperatures above 30°C or during rain to ensure herbicide efficacy and minimise runoff, unless specified otherwise.
  • Record weather conditions in Spray Diary.

5. Herbicide Check:

  • Confirm that the herbicide is approved for use in Victoria and suitable for the target area.
  • Inspect containers for labels, seals, and expiration dates.
  • Use Task Tracker to log inventory withdrawal and track herbicide usage.

4. Operation

1. Mixing Herbicides:

  • Mix in a designated area with spill containment measures.
  • Wear PPE as indicated on the label.
  • Fill the Toro 5800 tank halfway with water, then add the herbicide to reduce splashing.
  • Agitate the mixture using the Toro 5800’s built-in agitation system to ensure thorough mixing.
  • Never mix more herbicide than needed for the application.

2. Application:

  • Adjust the boom height and nozzle spacing on the Toro 5800 sprayer according to the herbicide’s coverage requirements.
  • Use the GPS-based control system to track spray patterns and ensure accurate, even application.
  • Maintain a consistent ground speed and pressure as per calibration.
  • Begin spraying from the centre of the area, avoiding sensitive boundaries such as waterways, trees, or neighbouring properties.
  • Overlap spray passes slightly for consistent coverage, avoiding double-spraying to prevent over-application.

3. Minimising Drift and Runoff:

  • Use drift-reduction nozzles if required.
  • Operate the sprayer at low pressure and avoid spraying in windy conditions.
  • Apply buffer zones as indicated on the label, particularly near water sources or sensitive areas.

4. Record Keeping:

  • Log all spray details in Spray Diary, including:
  • Herbicide used, rate of application, and total area covered.
  • Operator details, date, time, and weather conditions.
  • Calibration settings and any deviations from standard procedure.
  • Track remaining inventory in Task Tracker to ensure stock levels are accurate.

5. Post-Operational Procedures

1. Cleaning the Sprayer:

  • Use the Toro 5800’s self-cleaning system to rinse the tank, lines, and nozzles with clean water immediately after use.
  • Dispose of rinse water per herbicide label and EPA Victoria guidelines to prevent contamination.

2. Inspection and Maintenance:

  • Inspect the boom structure, nozzles, and hoses for wear or damage, recording any maintenance actions in Task Tracker.
  • Check and recalibrate the GPS system as needed to ensure precision for future applications.

3. Storage:

  • Store the sprayer in a clean, dry location away from ignition sources.
  • Secure herbicides in their original containers in a locked, ventilated storage area.

6. Emergency Procedures

1. Spill Management:

  • Contain the spill using absorbent materials or spill kits.
  • Notify the supervisor and dispose of contaminated materials as outlined in the herbicide SDS.
  • Record the incident in Spray Diary and Task Tracker.

2. Chemical Exposure:

  • For skin contact: Remove contaminated clothing and wash with soap and water for 15 minutes.
  • For inhalation: Move to fresh air and seek medical attention if symptoms persist.
  • For eye contact: Flush with water for 15 minutes and consult a doctor.
  • Document the incident in the club’s incident report system.

3. Equipment Malfunction:

  • Stop the sprayer immediately if leaks or pressure issues occur.
  • Tag the sprayer as "Out of Service" and report it to the maintenance team for repair.

7. Relevant Legislation and Guidelines

  • Agricultural and Veterinary Chemicals (Control of Use) Act 1992 (Vic): Governs safe use and application of herbicides.
  • EPA Victoria Guidelines: Covers spray drift management, buffer zones, and runoff prevention.
  • WorkSafe Victoria Standards: Mandates workplace safety, including PPE and chemical handling.
  • APVMA Guidelines: Regulates approved herbicides and labelling requirements.

8. Record Keeping

1. Spray Diary:

  • Log every application, including operator details, herbicides used, and environmental conditions.
  • Maintain these records for a minimum of five years to comply with Victorian law.

2. Task Tracker:

  • Track consumables, including herbicide inventory, usage, and reorder needs.
  • Use to forecast stock requirements and prevent overordering or wastage.
