Wash Down Bay

SWP Issue: Nov 2026 Review: Nov 2027
Description: Proper Use of Wash Down Bay
Make / Model: Triple Interceptor Wash Down Bay
⚠ DO NOT operate this equipment unless you have been fully trained in its safe use and operation, have read the user manual, and have conducted a risk assessment using the Task Tracker QR system.

1. Potential Hazards

  • Chemical Exposure: Risk of skin contact, inhalation, or ingestion of hazardous chemicals during mixing or rinsing.
  • Environmental Contamination: Mismanagement of chemicals or wash water can harm soil, groundwater, or waterways.
  • Slips, Trips, and Falls: Wet or oily surfaces increase the risk of accidents.
  • Pressure Washer Hazards: High-pressure water can cause injuries or damage equipment if misused.
  • Inadequate Drainage or Blockage: Build-up in the triple interceptor system may lead to overflow or contamination.

2. Required Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

  • Chemical-resistant gloves.
  • Long-sleeve shirts and long pants.
  • Safety goggles or a face shield.
  • Respirator or dust mask for handling fine chemical particles.
  • Steel-capped boots with slip-resistant soles.
  • High-visibility clothing during operations.

3. Pre-Operational Checks

1. Wash-Down Bay Inspection:

  • Ensure the triple interceptor system is clean and functional, with no blockages or leaks.
  • Inspect drainage grates for debris or damage.
  • Verify that spill kits and chemical storage areas are well-stocked and accessible.

2. Equipment Checks:

  • Check the pressure washer for wear, leaks, or loose fittings.
  • Inspect hoses and nozzles for damage or blockages.
  • Ensure all rinse tanks are clean and free from residue.

3. Chemical Handling Area:

  • Verify that the mixing area is clean and equipped with spill containment measures.
  • Confirm that all chemical containers are sealed, properly labelled, and stored in compliance with regulations.
  • Review Safety Data Sheets (SDS) for each chemical to ensure correct handling.

4. PPE Readiness:

  • Ensure all required PPE is in good condition and fits properly.

4. Operation

1. Chemical Mixing:

  • Use the designated chemical mixing area within the wash-down bay.
  • Read and follow the label instructions for all chemicals being used, including specific PPE requirements.
  • Add water to the rinse tank first, then introduce chemicals to reduce splashing.
  • Never mix incompatible chemicals to avoid dangerous reactions.
  • Keep containers sealed when not in use.

2. Spray Tank Rinsing:

  • Position the sprayer over the wash-down grate.
  • Use clean water to rinse tanks, ensuring no chemical residue remains.
  • Direct all rinse water into the triple interceptor system.
  • Monitor drainage to ensure proper flow through the system.

3. Machinery Washing:

  • Remove loose debris from machinery before using the wash-down bay to minimise contamination.
  • Use the pressure washer at the appropriate setting to avoid damage to equipment.
  • Clean undercarriages and hard-to-reach areas thoroughly.
  • Avoid directing water onto sensitive electronic components or fuel systems.

4. Environmental Protection:

  • Emphasise the importance of preventing contamination of soil and watercourses.
  • Ensure all wash water and residues are captured by the triple interceptor system.
  • Follow EPA Victoria guidelines for proper disposal of interceptor waste.

5. Post-Operational Procedures

1. Cleaning the Wash-Down Bay:

  • The wash-down bay must be always kept clean. Operators must clean up any spills, debris, or residues immediately after use.
  • It is the responsibility of the person assigned to lunchroom duty to ensure the wash-down bay is being maintained to these standards.
  • Rinse the wash-down bay floor to remove any chemical residues or dirt.

2. Hose Management:

  • Hoses must be wound up neatly after each use to prevent trip hazards and maintain a tidy workspace.

3. Inspecting the Interceptor System:

  • Check for blockages or excessive build-up of sludge or oil in the triple interceptor system.
  • Schedule routine maintenance, including pump-outs, as per the manufacturer’s guidelines.

4. Chemical Storage:

  • Return unused chemicals to their designated storage area.
  • Inspect containers for damage or leaks before storage.

5. Record Keeping:

  • Log chemical usage and waste disposal in Task Tracker or the designated record-keeping system.
  • Note any issues or maintenance needs for the wash-down bay.

6. Emergency Procedures

1. Spill Management:

  • Use spill containment kits immediately to control and clean up spills.
  • Notify the supervisor of any significant spills or leaks.
  • Dispose of contaminated materials according to EPA Victoria guidelines.

2. Chemical Exposure:

  • For skin contact, wash affected areas with soap and water for at least 15 minutes.
  • For eye contact, flush with clean water for 15 minutes and seek medical attention.
  • If inhaled, move to fresh air and seek medical assistance if symptoms persist.

3. System Overflow or Malfunction:

  • Stop operations and isolate the affected area.
  • Notify maintenance personnel immediately.
  • Do not resume operations until the issue is resolved.

7. Relevant Legislation and Guidelines

  • Environment Protection Act 2017 (Vic): Covers chemical handling, waste disposal, and prevention of environmental harm.
  • EPA Victoria Guidelines: Outlines standards for wash-down bays, including triple interceptor systems.
  • WorkSafe Victoria Standards: Mandates workplace safety, including PPE and equipment use.
  • Dangerous Goods (Storage and Handling) Regulations 2012 (Vic): Ensures safe storage and handling of hazardous substances.

8. Record Keeping

1. Task Tracker:

  • Log all chemical usage, waste disposal, and maintenance activities for the wash-down bay.

2. Maintenance Records:

  • Document interceptor pump-outs and servicing schedules.
  • Keep records of inspections and any corrective actions taken.
