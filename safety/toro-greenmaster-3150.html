Toro Greenmaster 3150

SWP Issue: Nov 2026 Review: Nov 2027
Description: Ride-On Tees and Approaches Mowing
Make / Model: Toro Greenmaster 3150
⚠ DO NOT operate this equipment unless you have been fully trained in its safe use and operation, have read the user manual, and have conducted a risk assessment using the Task Tracker QR system.

1. Potential Hazards

  • Rotating cutting reels: Three precision reels spin at high speed. Contact with an exposed reel can cause severe lacerations or finger amputation.
  • Rollover on slopes: The machine is lighter than a fairway mower and can tip on steep banks around tees and green surrounds, particularly on wet turf.
  • Noise exposure: Engine noise typically exceeds 85 dB(A), causing hearing damage with prolonged unprotected exposure.
  • Fuel and hydraulic fluid spills: Leaking fuel or hydraulic oil on tee surfaces and green surrounds causes turf damage and is an environmental contaminant.
  • Vibration: Whole-body vibration during extended operation can lead to lower back discomfort.
  • Struck by golf balls: Tee complexes are active play areas; operators are at risk of being struck by errant shots from adjacent tees or fairways.
  • Pinch points: Reel lift arms, seat adjustment, and engine compartment components present crush and pinch hazards.
  • Ejected debris: Ball markers, broken tees, and stones can be thrown from under cutting units at high velocity.
  • Hot engine components: Exhaust manifold and muffler can cause burns on contact during or after operation.

2. Required Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

  • Steel-capped or composite-toe boots with non-slip soles (mandatory).
  • Hearing protection rated Class 5 (mandatory).
  • Safety glasses (mandatory).
  • Sun hat and SPF 50+ sunscreen.
  • High-visibility vest during early morning or low-light starts.

3. Pre-Operational Checks

a. Machine Inspection

  • Complete pre-start checklist via Task Tracker QR system.
  • Check engine oil, hydraulic fluid level, and fuel level.
  • Inspect all three cutting reels for damage, sharpness, and bedknife contact. Adjust as needed.
  • Verify height-of-cut settings match the superintendent's daily instruction for tees.
  • Check tyre pressures; uneven pressures create an uneven cut across the three units.
  • Inspect grass catchers for tears, secure attachment, and clean interior.
  • Test the operator-presence system: engine must stop when the operator leaves the seat with reels engaged.

b. Work Area

  • Walk each tee complex before mowing to remove debris, broken tees, divot mix containers, and ball markers.
  • Identify sprinkler heads, yardage markers, and tee signs to avoid contact.
  • Check for golfers on the tee or approaching; coordinate via two-way radio.

4. Operation

a. Start-Up

  • Sit in the operator seat. Ensure reels are disengaged and the park brake is applied.
  • Start the engine and allow to warm for 30 seconds.
  • Engage reels only when positioned on the mowing area, not on paths or hard surfaces.

b. During Operation

  • Mow at a consistent speed for a uniform cut. Do not rush turns on small tee surfaces.
  • Follow the daily mowing direction; alternate direction with each mow to prevent grain.
  • Lift reels before turning at the end of each pass to prevent scuffing.
  • Empty grass catchers before they are three-quarters full.
  • On tee banks, mow up and down the slope rather than across where the gradient is steep.

c. Golf Course Specific Rules

  • Stop mowing immediately when golfers are on the tee or preparing to play. Move clear of the tee complex.
  • Do not mow over tee markers or yardage plates. Lift the deck or steer around them.
  • Report any scalping, oil leaks on turf, or sprinkler head damage to the superintendent immediately.
  • Dispose of clippings in the designated area, not in bunkers or native zones.

5. Emergency Procedures

a. Fire

  • Stop the engine immediately. Dismount and move upwind.
  • Use the nearest fire extinguisher if the fire is small.
  • Call 000 for uncontrolled fire. Notify the superintendent via radio.

b. Injury

  • Stop the machine, disengage reels, turn off the engine.
  • Administer first aid. For reel or bedknife cuts, apply direct pressure.
  • Call 000 for serious injuries. Site: 825 Cranbourne-Frankston Rd, Cranbourne VIC 3977.
  • Report in Task Tracker.

6. Post-Operational Procedures

  • Disengage reels and allow to stop. Apply park brake.
  • Drive to the wash-down bay at reduced speed.
  • Wash thoroughly, paying attention to cutting units and underside.
  • Empty and rinse grass catchers.
  • Inspect reels and bedknives for damage.
  • Refuel if below half tank.
  • Park in the designated bay. Report faults in Task Tracker.

7. Maintenance and Servicing

  • Daily: clean machine, check oil and reel condition.
  • Weekly: grease zerks, check belt tension, inspect air filter.
  • Reel backlapping as directed by the superintendent.
  • All servicing recorded in Task Tracker with hours and parts.

8. Relevant Legislation

  • Occupational Health and Safety Act 2004 (Vic) - duties of employers and employees.
  • Occupational Health and Safety Regulations 2017 (Vic) - plant and equipment, noise, manual handling.
  • AS/NZS 4024.1 Safety of Machinery series.
  • EPA Victoria noise regulations - Environment Protection Act 2017.
  • Dangerous Goods (Storage and Handling) Regulations 2012 (Vic) where applicable.

9. Record Keeping

  • Pre-start checklists completed daily via Task Tracker QR system.
  • All faults, repairs, and servicing recorded in Task Tracker.
  • Incident and near-miss reports submitted through Task Tracker.
  • Training records maintained for all operators with superintendent sign-off.
