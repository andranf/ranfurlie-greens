Backpack Blower

SWP Issue: Nov 2026 Review: Nov 2027
Description: Using a Backpack Blower
Make / Model: Stihl / Kress / Milwaukee Backpack Blowers
⚠ DO NOT operate this equipment unless you have been fully trained in its safe use and operation, have read the user manual, and have conducted a risk assessment using the Task Tracker QR system.

1. Potential Hazards

  • Noise Exposure: Combustion blowers emit high-decibel noise that can cause hearing damage and disturb residents, particularly during restricted hours.
  • Exhaust Fumes: Combustion engines release carbon monoxide and other toxic gases, posing respiratory risks in enclosed spaces or poor ventilation.
  • Fire Risk: Combustion engines and lithium-ion batteries present a fire hazard due to flammable fuels, battery overheating, or electrical faults.
  • Chemical Exposure: Fuel, oil, and leaking battery chemicals can cause skin irritation, burns, or environmental contamination.
  • Electrical Hazards: Faulty chargers or damaged lithium batteries can cause electric shock or fires.
  • Flying Debris: High-velocity airflow can propel debris, potentially injuring operators or bystanders.
  • Thermal Burns: Hot engine components, exhausts, or overheated batteries can cause burns upon contact.
  • Explosion Risk: Overcharged, punctured, or overheated lithium-ion batteries can release gases that ignite, causing explosions.
  • Ergonomic Strain: Improper strap adjustments or prolonged use can lead to back, neck, or shoulder strain.
  • Slip, Trip, and Fall: Uneven terrain or wet surfaces can increase the risk of accidents while using the equipment.
  • Environmental Harm: Improper disposal of fuels, oils, or batteries can damage ecosystems and violate legal regulations.
  • Mechanical Failures: Faulty equipment, such as blocked air intakes or jammed fans, can result in sudden loss of control or operation.

2. Required Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

  • Mandatory:
  • Hearing Protection: Earplugs or earmuffs (for all blower types, especially combustion models).
  • Eye Protection: Safety glasses with side shields to protect against flying debris.
  • Gloves: Heat-resistant gloves for combustion blowers and chemical-resistant gloves for handling batteries.
  • Footwear: Steel-capped boots with slip-resistant soles.
  • Clothing: High-visibility clothing, long pants, and long sleeves to protect against scratches and sun exposure.
  • Recommended:
  • Dust mask or respirator in dry, dusty conditions.
  • Fire-resistant gloves for handling lithium batteries in the event of overheating.

3. Pre-Operational Checks

1. Inspection of the Blower:

  • Combustion Models:
  • Check for fuel or oil leaks.
  • Inspect the fuel tank and lines for damage.
  • Verify the air filter and spark plug are clean and functional.
  • Lithium-Ion Models:
  • Inspect the battery for swelling, cracks, or physical damage.
  • Check the charging port and connections for debris or corrosion.

2. Noise Restrictions:

  • Combustion Models:
  • Do not operate near boundaries before 7:00am on weekdays or 9:00am on weekends and public holidays, as per EPA noise restrictions.
  • Plan operations accordingly to minimise disturbance.
  • Lithium-Ion Models:
  • Preferred for early operations due to lower noise levels.

3. Area Preparation:

  • Walk the work area and remove any objects that may become projectiles.
  • Identify potential hazards such as flammable materials, uneven terrain, or obstructions.
  • Ensure the area is clear of bystanders, animals, and vehicles.

4. Fuelling and Charging:

  • Combustion Models:
  • Refuel outdoors with the engine off and cool.
  • Avoid overfilling the tank and clean up any spills immediately.
  • Lithium-Ion Models:
  • Charge batteries in a well-ventilated, fire-resistant location.
  • Use only manufacturer-approved chargers.

5. Documentation and Competency:

  • Verify the operator has completed training for the equipment.
  • Record all pre-start checks in the equipment logbook.

4. Operation

1. Starting the Blower:

  • For combustion models: Start the engine on a flat surface, ensuring the throttle is at idle and the blower is stable.
  • For lithium-ion models: Securely connect the battery and power on the blower according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

2. During Use:

  • Use the blower at the lowest effective throttle to reduce noise and fuel consumption.
  • Maintain a safe distance from bystanders and direct airflow away from them.
  • Avoid directing airflow at fragile objects, loose gravel, or sensitive areas.

3. Ergonomic Safety:

  • Adjust shoulder straps to evenly distribute the weight of the blower.
  • Maintain an upright posture to reduce back and shoulder strain.
  • Take breaks every 30 minutes to prevent fatigue.

4. Noise Management:

  • Use combustion blowers only during permitted hours and in compliance with noise restrictions.
  • Choose lithium-ion blowers where feasible to minimise noise impacts.

5. Emergency Shutdown:

  • Turn off the blower immediately if abnormal sounds, excessive vibration, or overheating occurs.
  • Move the blower to a safe location and inspect it for issues before restarting.

5. Post-Operational Procedures

1. Shutdown:

  • For combustion models: Allow the engine to cool before refuelling or storing.
  • For lithium-ion models: Disconnect the battery after use.

2. Cleaning and Inspection:

  • Clean debris from the air intake, nozzle, and housing.
  • Inspect straps, fasteners, and other components for wear or damage.

3. Storage:

  • Combustion Models: Store in a cool, ventilated area away from ignition sources.
  • Lithium-Ion Models: Store batteries in a cool, dry place, ideally at 40–80% charge.

4. Reporting Issues:

  • Report any faults, damage, or unusual performance to the supervisor immediately.
  • Record post-operation inspections in the logbook.

6. Maintenance and Disposal

1. Routine Maintenance:

  • Combustion Models: Clean air filters, replace spark plugs as needed, and check exhaust systems for blockages.
  • Lithium-Ion Models: Test batteries regularly for capacity and ensure chargers are clean and functional.

2. Disposal:

  • Dispose of lithium-ion batteries at authorised recycling centres.
  • Safely dispose of old fuel and oil in compliance with local environmental regulations.

7. Emergency Procedures

1. Fire Response:

  • For combustion blowers: Use a Class B fire extinguisher.
  • For lithium-ion blowers: Use a Class D extinguisher or smother with sand.

2. Chemical Exposure:

  • Rinse skin or eyes with water if exposed to fuel or battery chemicals.
  • Seek medical attention for persistent irritation or injury.

3. Flying Debris or Injuries:

  • Administer first aid for cuts or abrasions caused by debris.
  • Call emergency services for serious injuries.

4. Overheating or Explosion:

  • Shut off the blower immediately and move it to a safe area.
  • Do not attempt to puncture or cool batteries with water.

8. Relevant Legislation and Standards

  • Occupational Health and Safety Act 2004 (Vic): Obligations to ensure a safe workplace.
  • Environmental Protection Act 2017 (Vic): Compliance with EPA noise and environmental regulations.
  • AS/NZS 60745.2.13: Standards for handheld motor-operated tools.
