Toro Hover Pro 450

SWP Issue: Nov 2026 Review: Nov 2027
Description: Hover Mowing of Banks and Slopes
Make / Model: Toro Hover Pro 450
⚠ DO NOT operate this equipment unless you have been fully trained in its safe use and operation, have read the user manual, and have conducted a risk assessment using the Task Tracker QR system.

1. Potential Hazards

  • Rotating blade: Blade spins at high speed under a lightweight deck with no wheel contact on the ground. The hovering action means less stability than a wheeled mower.
  • Slope hazard: Hover mowers are used specifically on steep banks and slopes where other mowers cannot operate. The operator is at constant risk of slipping and placing a foot under the hovering deck.
  • Ejected debris: Objects flung from under the deck can strike the operator at close range, particularly on rough terrain with hidden rocks.
  • Noise exposure: Petrol engine exceeds 90 dB(A).
  • Vibration: Significant hand-arm vibration during extended use on rough terrain.
  • Exhaust fumes: Carbon monoxide risk, especially in sheltered areas such as tree-lined banks.
  • Manual handling: The mower is carried to and from the work site and lifted over obstacles, risking strain.
  • Fuel fire: Refuelling on slopes creates spill risk; fuel running downhill toward drains or waterways.

2. Required Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

  • Steel-capped boots with aggressive tread/non-slip soles (mandatory - critical on slopes).
  • Hearing protection rated Class 5 (mandatory).
  • Safety glasses or face shield (mandatory).
  • Long trousers (mandatory).
  • Sun hat and SPF 50+ sunscreen.
  • Gloves for handling the blade and during maintenance.

3. Pre-Operational Checks

a. Machine Inspection

  • Complete pre-start checklist via Task Tracker QR system.
  • Check engine oil level.
  • Inspect the blade for sharpness, cracks, and secure bolt torque. A loose blade on a hover mower is extremely dangerous.
  • Check the blade brake clutch: blade must stop within 3 seconds of bail release.
  • Inspect the air cushion skirt for tears or damage that would reduce hover performance.
  • Check fuel level; refuel on flat ground only.

b. Work Area

  • Inspect the slope for rabbit holes, exposed roots, rocks, and irrigation heads.
  • Check the slope angle; slopes steeper than 1:3 require a risk assessment and may need a harness system.
  • Ensure the area below the slope is clear of people, as debris rolls and flies downhill.
  • Wet conditions increase slip risk dramatically. Consider postponing if the slope is saturated.

4. Operation

a. Start-Up

  • Place the mower on flat ground to start. Feet clear of the deck.
  • Start engine, allow to warm, then carry or walk the mower to the slope.

b. During Operation

  • Always mow across the slope, never directly up and down.
  • Keep the mower below you on the slope; never stand below a hover mower.
  • Use smooth, sweeping strokes. Do not jerk or yank the mower.
  • Keep both hands on the handle at all times.
  • If you lose footing, let go of the bail lever immediately so the blade stops.
  • Take regular breaks; slope mowing is physically demanding and fatigue increases slip risk.

c. Golf Course Specific Rules

  • Mow slopes and banks early in the day before heavy foot traffic.
  • Keep clear of bunker faces; blade contact with sand lips damages both turf and the blade.
  • Do not hover-mow around active tee or green areas while golfers are present.

5. Emergency Procedures

a. Fire

  • Stop engine. Move mower away from fuel. Use extinguisher. Call 000 if uncontrolled.

b. Injury

  • Stop machine, disengage blade. Administer first aid.
  • Call 000 for serious injuries. Site: 825 Cranbourne-Frankston Rd, Cranbourne VIC 3977.
  • Report in Task Tracker.

c. Equipment-Specific Emergency

  • If operator slips on a slope, let go of the bail lever immediately. Do not try to hold the mower.
  • If the mower slides down a slope uncontrolled, do not chase it. Wait for it to stop and the blade to stop spinning.

6. Post-Operational Procedures

  • Disengage blade, allow to stop, turn off engine.
  • Carry the mower back to flat ground; do not drag it.
  • Clean at the wash-down bay.
  • Park in designated bay. Report faults in Task Tracker.

7. Maintenance and Servicing

  • Daily: clean, check oil, inspect blade and skirt.
  • Weekly: sharpen blade, inspect air filter.
  • Replace skirt when worn to maintain hover efficiency.
  • All servicing recorded in Task Tracker.

8. Relevant Legislation

  • Occupational Health and Safety Act 2004 (Vic).
  • Occupational Health and Safety Regulations 2017 (Vic).
  • AS/NZS 4024.1 Safety of Machinery series.
  • EPA Victoria noise regulations - Environment Protection Act 2017.
  • Dangerous Goods (Storage and Handling) Regulations 2012 (Vic) where applicable.

9. Record Keeping

  • Pre-start checklists completed daily via Task Tracker QR system.
  • All faults, repairs, and servicing recorded in Task Tracker.
  • Incident and near-miss reports submitted through Task Tracker.
  • Training records maintained for all operators with superintendent sign-off.
