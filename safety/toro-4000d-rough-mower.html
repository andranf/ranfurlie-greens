Toro 4000D Rough Mower

SWP Issue: Nov 2026 Review: Nov 2027
Description: Operating a Rough Mower
Make / Model: Toro 4000D
⚠ DO NOT operate this equipment unless you have been fully trained in its safe use and operation, have read the user manual, and have conducted a risk assessment using the Task Tracker QR system.

1. Potential Hazards

  • Entanglement: Moving parts, belts, and blades.
  • Impact and Cutting Injuries: Rotating blades and projectiles.
  • Noise and Vibration: Prolonged exposure risks.
  • Overturning: Steep slopes or uneven terrain.
  • Crush Injuries: Roll-over or mishandling.
  • Fire Hazard: Hot surfaces, fuel spills, and leaks.
  • Chemical Exposure: Fuel, oil, and hydraulic fluids.
  • Environmental Hazards: Slopes, sand traps, or water bodies.

2. Required Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

  • Mandatory:
  • Steel-capped boots with slip-resistant soles.
  • High-visibility clothing.
  • Hearing protection (earmuffs or plugs).
  • Safety glasses.
  • Gloves for maintenance tasks.
  • Recommended:
  • Dust mask (for dry, dusty conditions).
  • Sunscreen and a wide-brimmed hat for UV protection.

3. Pre-Operational Checks

1. Inspection of the Mower:

  • Check oil, fuel, and hydraulic fluid levels; top up as needed.
  • Inspect tyres for correct inflation, wear, or damage.
  • Ensure all safety guards and decals are present and legible.
  • Inspect cutting blades for sharpness, cracks, or damage.
  • Ensure no loose or missing fasteners, hoses, or components.
  • Verify lights, reverse buzzer, horn, and other indicators are functioning.

2. Area Preparation:

  • Walk the area to be mowed, removing debris such as rocks, sticks, and rubbish.
  • Identify hazardous areas such as steep slopes, sand traps, water bodies, or uneven terrain.

3. Controls and Safety Features:

  • Test brakes (service and park), steering, and throttle.
  • Confirm seatbelt operation.
  • Check the Roll-Overprotective Structure (ROPS) is in place and not damaged.
  • Ensure the dead man’s switch functions correctly.

4. Documentation and Competency:

  • Ensure operator has completed training and is authorised to use the equipment.
  • Record pre-start inspections in the equipment logbook.
  • Conduct a site-specific risk assessment.

5. Fuel Safety:

  • Refuel outdoors with the engine off and cool.
  • Avoid overfilling the tank.
  • Immediately clean spills and dispose of rags appropriately.

4. Operation

1. Starting the Engine:

  • Ensure the park brake is engaged, and the cutting units are disengaged.
  • Set throttle to neutral before starting the engine.
  • Start the engine from the operator's seat only.

2. Mowing:

  • Set the cutting height according to the course requirements.
  • Engage cutting units only on flat ground and at low RPM.
  • Avoid mowing in reverse unless necessary and ensure the area is clear.
  • Always operate the mower at a speed appropriate to the terrain.

3. Navigating Slopes and Hazards:

  • Do not mow across slopes; mow up and down instead.
  • Avoid sudden starts, stops, or sharp turns on slopes.
  • Reduce speed near hazards like bunkers, water bodies, and ditches.
  • Use extreme caution when operating close to obstacles.

4. During Operation:

  • Stop immediately if you hit a foreign object or experience unusual vibrations.
  • Inspect the mower and repair any damage before continuing.
  • Always remain seated while the mower is running.
  • Keep bystanders at least 15 metres away from the mowing area.

5. Emergency Procedures

1. Fire or Hydraulic Leak:

  • Shut down the engine and move to a safe location.
  • Use a fire extinguisher for small fires, call emergency services for larger fires.
  • Do not attempt to repair hydraulic leaks under pressure.

2. Roll-Over:

  • Stay within the confines of the ROPS and seatbelt during a roll-over.
  • Call for assistance and do not attempt to move the mower until inspected.

3. Accidents or Injuries:

  • Administer first aid and call emergency services if required.
  • Report the incident to the supervisor immediately and complete an incident report.

6. Post-Operational Procedures

1. Shutdown:

  • Disengage cutting units and reduce the throttle to idle.
  • Park on a flat surface, engage the park brake, and turn off the engine.
  • Remove the key from the ignition.

2. Cleaning and Maintenance:

  • Use compressed air or water to clean grass clippings from cutting units.
  • Avoid spraying water into electrical components or hydraulic systems.
  • Inspect the mower for damage, wear, or leaks.
  • Refuel and document usage in the logbook.

3. Storage:

  • Park the mower in the designated area, ensuring cutting units are lowered.
  • Securely close the fuel tank and store it away from ignition sources.

7. Maintenance

1. Routine Maintenance:

  • Follow the manufacturer’s maintenance schedule.
  • Replace worn or damaged blades as a set to maintain balance.
  • Inspect hydraulic lines regularly and replace if frayed or cracked.
  • Keep the engine and radiator free from grass and debris to prevent overheating.

2. Safety Considerations:

  • Relieve hydraulic pressure before servicing the system.
  • Use proper tools and follow lockout/tagout procedures.

8. Relevant Legislation

  • Occupational Health and Safety Act 2004 (Vic): Obligations to maintain a safe workplace.
  • Dangerous Goods (Storage and Handling) Regulations 2012 (Vic): Safe handling of flammable liquids.
  • AS/NZS 4024.1 Safety of Machinery: Standards for safe design and operation.
  • Environmental Protection Act 2017 (Vic): Guidelines for spill and waste management.
